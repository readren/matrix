I've been analyzing the `CausalFence` implementation in `Doer.scala`, and I see exactly why you're concerned. The "sequential updater" invariant (line 1100) and the way `step` (line 1256) is implemented create a strict bottleneck:

1.  Each `advance` or `advanceIf` enqueues a new `Covenant` that is the **tail** of the causal chain.
2.  The next transition (or even a `causalAnchor` for network replication) cannot see the updated state until the `newStateProviderDuty` from the previous `advance` is fulfilled.
3.  If that `Duty` includes a local disk write, the entire causal chain is stalled until the disk acknowledges. This prevents the network replication from even "starting" for that record until it's already durable locally.

### How to achieve parallel appending without losing the `CausalFence`

To keep the `CausalFence` and its "game-changing invariant" (which ensures all derived states see a consistent sequence), you need to separate **In-Memory Visibility** from **Durable Commitment**.

Here is an architectural pattern that could work:

**1. The "In-Memory" Primary State**
Redefine your `PrimaryState` to represent the **In-Memory Log**.
- The `advanceIf` for a new command should perform the in-memory append **instantly**.
- It should return a `ReadyDuty(newState)`.
- This fulfills the `CausalFence` link immediately.

**2. The "Durable" and "Replicated" Derived States**
Now, the network replication AND the local disk write become **derived processes** that anchor to the fence:
- **Network:** Anchors to the latest record and starts sending.
- **Disk:** Also anchors to the latest record and starts the asynchronous write.
- **Consensus Logic:** Only moves the `commitIndex` forward (another `advanceIf`) when it receives a majority of acknowledgments, where the "Local Disk Ack" is treated just like a "Follower Ack."

**Why this preserves the CausalFence:**
- The **deterministic lineage** of the log is still perfectly preserved.
- Any derived state (like the `Leader` logic) still sees the records in the exact order they were appended.
- The `CausalFence` continues to provide the "Single-threaded execution" guarantee for your core logic.

**The "Risk":**
If the leader crashes before the disk write finishes, the "In-Memory" state is lost. But this is standard Raft! If the record wasn't committed (because it wasn't durable on a majority), it's okay if it disappears. If it WAS durable on a majority of followers but not the leader, a new leader will eventually overwrite it or the old leader will catch up.
